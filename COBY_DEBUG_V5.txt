3. NUKE RULES — CURRENT SOURCE DESIGN
==================================================
Rarity:
- weight 3.
- actual pickup reroll extends Phase 6C pool.
- pre-final total pool weight 126.

Targeting:
- normal artillery projectile is a designator.
- its authoritative impact locks target.
- 850 ms warning follows target lock.
- beam visible approx 420 ms.

Beam:
- diagonal.
- half-length 520 world units.
- direct hit half-width 88.
- terrain strip approximated with overlapping deep cuts:
  radius 92, depth 2450, step 115.

Damage:
- 20 direct HP.
- self damage YES.
- friendly fire YES.
- Shield halves applicable hit and is consumed.
- conventional knockback NO.
- terrain-caused falling YES and intentional.

Pickups:
- beam destroys intersected pickups.
- destroyed pickups are not collected.
- Nuke does not trigger pickup-retains-turn.

Turn:
- Nuke always ends the turn after resolution if match continues.

4. IMPORTANT IMPLEMENTATION DECISIONS
==================================================
Why phase6d.js wrapper:
- preserve closed Phase 6C logic.
- minimize regression surface.
- isolate Nuke mechanics/tests.

Why designator projectile:
- reuse existing aim/power/wind/camera/projectile authority.
- no client-selected arbitrary target coordinate.
- no new targeting input protocol required.

Why separate frontend overlay canvas:
- an initial same-canvas wrapper approach could be erased by the base renderer's requestAnimationFrame/clear cycle.
- Phase 6D now draws Nuke-specific visuals on a transparent canvas layered above the main canvas.
- this architecture must be manually checked for camera alignment.

Why pickups are destroyed:
- Nuke is a disintegration weapon, not an explosion pickup collector.
- avoids inheriting the normal explosion pickup-retains-turn exception.
